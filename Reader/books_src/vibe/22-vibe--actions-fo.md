# Actions for Impact

To take a quiz about your vibes, visit [adamgrant.net](https://adamgrant.net/).

This is an idea book, not a self-help book, but I still hope it’s helpful. If you’re looking to bridge from insight to action, here are my top 40 practical takeaways.

### I. SPARK CONNECTION

  1. _Rethink your potential to vibe._ Recognizing people as possible associates, allies, pals, and companions opens new possibilities for connection. Fun pals aren’t necessarily the most compassionate companions, and caring companions aren’t always the most productive allies.




#### A. Build Compatibility

  1. _Find the right wavelength._ Don’t worry about whether you have compatible traits—look for compatible interests, needs, goals, and values. A vibe is not the energy someone gives off; it’s the energy you create together when your expectations are aligned.

  2. _Choose the right interaction ritual._ Face-to-face conversations are most useful for becoming associates and companions. Side-by-side activities are better for becoming allies and pals.

  3. _Exchange origin stories._ Sharing past experiences builds more meaningful bonds than discussing future goals. Personal histories reveal common ground.




#### B. Master Shape-Shifting

  1. _Second-guess your first impressions._ Beware of making strong assumptions based on small samples of behavior. Conclusions often overemphasize character and underemphasize circumstances.

  2. _Observe people across a range of settings._ People don’t express the same traits in every situation—different if-then signatures are activated by different situations. By paying attention to the patterns behind their shape-shifts, you can find contexts where you click.

  3. _Trade user manuals._ Tell people what brings out the best in you—and the worst. Ask them about their if-then tendencies too. When you understand what activates each other’s virtues and vices, it’s easier to vibe.

  4. _Seek out people who help you evolve._ Self-expansion fuels greater closeness than self-expression. Pursue pals with different interests to discover new hobbies, allies with different skills to develop new goals, and companions with different principles to identify new values.




#### C. Accelerate Attachment

  1. _Intensify your interactions._ The more you feel together, the faster you bond together. Whether it’s a scary bridge, a haunted house, a tough workout, or an improv comedy class, shared emotional experiences get your hearts racing—and your connection growing.

  2. _Dive into the deep end._ Skipping small talk can speed up connection. Ditch _What do you do?_ in favor of _What do you love to do?_ if you’re looking for pals, _What’s your biggest challenge?_ if you’re searching for allies, and _What’s one of your regrets?_ if you’re seeking companions.

  3. _Show bounded vulnerability._ Admitting a mistake, disclosing a flaw, and revealing genuine emotion can display humanity and signal trust. To avoid a trauma dump, start by sharing from your scars, not your wounds.




### II. SUSTAIN CONNECTION

  1. _Send pebbles to stay in touch._ Sharing personalized articles, links, and videos is not a trivial gesture. A little memory flex shows that you listened—and cared enough to remember. Pebbling is a bid for connection and an act of care. _I’m thinking of you_ is a message people appreciate receiving (and sending).




#### A. Initiate Intermittent Interaction

  1. _Welcome low-maintenance relationships._ Relax your expectations of regular contact—absence makes the heart grow fonder. Replace mundane catch-ups with interactions that are less frequent but more worthwhile, like a monthly game, sporadic pranks, an annual trip, or reunion parties.

  2. _Unleash burstiness._ Get fresh starts through temporary farewells. Time apart allows you to rethink, refuel, retool, and reconnect.

  3. _Rekindle lost connections_**.** Reconnecting with dormant ties is surprisingly enjoyable and beneficial. You can revive old bonds by sharing gratitude for a past shared experience, joy about a current milestone, or an invitation to a future group dinner.




#### B. Address Mixed Feelings

  1. _Accept the acceptable_**.** It’s better to tolerate minor vices than demand change. By validating others’ preferences, you convey that it’s okay for them to have thoughts, emotions, and habits that differ from yours.

  2. _Contain the unacceptable._ Clear boundaries tell people what conduct won’t be tolerated. It’s not enough to describe the behaviors—you also need to specify the situations in which they’re more and less problematic.

  3. _Weigh the good against the bad._ The test of whether a relationship is worth maintaining is not whether people have vices. It’s whether their vices can be contained or have hidden virtues.




#### C. Embrace Respectful Conflict

  1. _Fight on the right wavelength._ Healthy conflict is not you against them—it’s you and them against the problem.

  2. _Lead with your intention to help._ Be direct in what you say and kind in how and why you say it. Preface your feedback with your version of _I’m sharing this because I care about you and our relationship_ or _I’m giving you these comments because I have very high expectations and I know that you can reach them_.

  3. _Abandon the feedback sandwich._ Ditch the second slice of praise and serve an open-faced sandwich that clearly separates the positive and the negative. _I have notes on strengths and areas for growth—which would you prefer to hear first?_ Or go straight to the growth message: _You know what would make you even more effective?_

  4. _Walk down the ladder of inference._ Instead of jumping to conclusions about people’s intentions, ask them to explain their reasoning. _Can you help me understand how you saw the situation? I know I can’t read your mind, so would you be willing to read it to me?_




### III. SCALE CONNECTION

  1. _Bring community back._ Be a circle opener by introducing your favorite people to each other. Collective effervescence floods us with joy and hope, and groups build belonging in ways that individual bonds don’t.




#### A. Design Great Gatherings

  1. _Create a compelling purpose around a common wavelength._ Invite people to interact as pals through Admin Night or a Colonoscopy Party, companions through a Dinner Party for people grieving, or allies through the Reciprocity Ring, a “Why Do Our Customers Hate Our Product?” roundtable, or a Jeffersonian dinner.

  2. _Do a meeting audit._ Delete recurring meetings and ask which ones are really necessary. If you’re not gathering to bond, learn, create, or decide, you don’t need to gather.

  3. _Plan for serendipity._ Choreograph social events to foster connection. Give guests a list of recommended people to meet. Set the stage for them to become pals and allies through interactive exercises. Assign them to seats at round tables and move toward companionship with deeper questions.

  4. _Enforce clear norms._ Clarify what behaviors are off-limits—and announce a fun penalty if people break the rules. _Don’t talk to people you already know_ expands circles; _no devices_ focuses attention; _no advising, fixing, or solving_ encourages listening and support; and the fear of eating jelly beans that taste like barf and boogers can be enough to motivate compliance.




#### B. Create Distinctive Communities

  1. _Divide into unique groups._ Offer people a niche where they can fit in and stand out within a larger community. Try splitting schools into houses like Hogwarts or neighborhoods into hamlets with mailboxes clustered together.

  2. _Build belonging from the beginning._ Jump-start the vibe by welcoming people in advance. For new students, reduce belonging uncertainty by sharing letters from current students about how they worried early on about fitting in—and how they found their people. For new teammates, offer early onboarding by sending notes a few weeks before they arrive about your culture and their contribution. Then have them share their personal highlight reels with their new colleagues.

  3. _Give people distinctive roles._ Make people feel needed—not just included—by giving them a place and a purpose. Put students in a jigsaw classroom where each student is responsible for a puzzle piece in group projects. Invite employees to revise their own job descriptions and seek buy-in from their teams.

  4. _Nest groups together under a collective goal or interest._ Ditch the Sorting Hat and unite groups around a shared mission. Highlight what makes each group special, not superior. They don’t need a common enemy; they only need a common cause.




#### C. Get Squads in Sync

  1. _Identify core values._ Discover shared values by asking people to tell stories about defining moments in the squad. Rank your top four values together.

  2. _Map values to behaviors._ Achieve clarity by aligning on the specific actions that uphold each of your values—and what it looks like to violate them. Show respect by testing your pee and cleaning up popcorn in the break room.

  3. _Establish accountability._ Create consequences for living the values, not only performing tasks and roles. Give immediate feedback when people violate shared principles—and make the costs clear and consistent.

  4. _Screen out bad apples._ Don’t let toxic people onto the team. Beware of people who kiss up and kick down, play the victim, or admire people who violate your values.




#### D. Form and Reform Family Traditions

  1. _Spice up family dinners._ Nurture values by asking kids to count their contributions as well as their blessings. _Who did you help this week, and who helped you?_ Build belonging and resilience by telling stories about family struggles and successes. Family history doesn’t have to be a chore; you can turn family history into a trivia game or even use a timer to keep it light.

  2. _Invent new traditions_. Enhance closeness by creating and sustaining traditions together. Strengthen pal bonds over birthday cake breakfasts, T. rex costumes for airport pickups, or hiding Justin. Become better allies and companions by holding weekly or monthly family council meetings.

  3. _Broaden the family unit._ Create rituals with extended and chosen families. Shared experiences like Cousin Camp with grandparents or weekends with friends’ families can expand connection.

  4. _Prioritize pair bonding._ Carve out one-on-one time for parents without kids, each parent with each kid, and siblings without parents. Pair traditions build family foundations.

  5. _Normalize failure._ Some interactions will inevitably fall flat. Trial and error is how you learn to vibe with more of the people more of the time.
