# Acknowledgments

You can’t see all the interactions that shaped this book, but you can feel their impact. Over the past two years, I’ve been lucky to work with a world-class squad. I’m especially grateful to three people who were with me on each wavelength through every draft. Richard Pine, for always knowing what I’m trying to say, and never hesitating to tell me “say better.” Andrea Schulz, for engaging with alacrity and acuity, seeing the potential in the framework, and helping the ideas reach their potential. And Marissa Shandell, for refusing to settle for good enough, and being the best ally a writer could have.

I’m also thankful to my Avengers for being ready and willing to assemble. Grace Rubenstein vanquished confusion with clarity. Reb Rebele challenged me to think deeper and bring the big picture into sharper focus. Karren Knowlton pinpointed problems and proposed perceptive pivots and patches. Courtney Elliott and the Impact Lab helped me rethink the framing and enliven the examples. Justin Berg reshaped the primal mark, and Matt Shirley created some epic charts. Paul Durbin meticulously scrutinized each study, story, and sentence (any mistakes are mine alone). Stacey Kalish scoured the world and the web for relevant anecdotes and helped me get to the heart of them. And Susan Grant corrected my grammar and showed me the importance of having a lifelong fan.

The teams at Viking and InkWell have gone above and beyond to make this book a reality. Shoutouts to the vision and passion of Brian Tart and Kate Stark; the steadfast publicity drumbeat from Carolyn Coleburn, Whitney Peeling, and Julia Falkner; the innovative marketing initiatives spearheaded by Mary Stone and Isabella Basile; the original (and obliging) artistic efforts of Pete Garceau and Jason Ramirez; the design taste of Daniel Lagin; the editorial, administrative, and production support from Elizabeth Pham Janowski, Tricia Conley, Susan VanHecke, and Eric Wechter; the audio tapestry stitched together by Julie Wilson, Lauren Klein, and Travis Tonn; and all the work behind the scenes by Alexis Hurley, Nathaniel Jacks, and Eliza Rothstein.

This book wouldn’t exist without a handful of people who I can never say enough good things about. Hats off to Jessica Gelman for always noticing, Jen Garner for stepping up, and Brian Little for changing my life and worldview. And I feel fortunate that day by day, my family has cultivated the vibes I’ve been trying to capture. Allison offered constant companionship and timely bursts of ideas, helping me get unstuck more times than I can count. Joanna shared poignant pebbles, protected against palvaders, and taught me to connect with quiet confidence. Henry invented new rituals, leading the pal charge from races to riddles and the screens to the slopes. And Elena inspired the title even though she doesn’t like it.
